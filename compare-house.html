The holes, the House's own

Thirteen sheets from 2009 to 2017 carry the Clerk's tally and no names, because their letters could not be read to a count that matched the tally, and nine roll calls of 2020 are listed without names in the legislature's own feed. They are left out of every rate above.

Four sheets carry dates that run backwards against their numbers. On those four the Speaker's row, and any name that needs the date to tell two members apart, is left unjoined, which is five votes.

The Speaker rarely votes, so the Speaker does not reach the sixty votes a rate needs and does not appear in the tables. Three members share a state member code with someone who held it earlier, and appear in the download under a code with the court added, such as CJW1-193.

The legislature's roster for the 186th General Court carries no party for anyone, and nothing is carried back from a later court, because people change parties. So no party rate above uses a vote from 2009 or 2010, and a member who served only then shows no party. Every other rate counts those years in full.

A member appears here only with at least 100 roll calls, and a rate is shown only where it rests on at least 60 votes. Below that a percentage is noise rather than a comparison.
