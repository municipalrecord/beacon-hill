Where the state's feed falls short

Every Senate roll call here is read from the Clerk's printed sheet, which names who voted, who was absent or not voting, who answered present, and who was paired. The legislature's data feed leaves out most absences, has no present or paired names at all, and in the 188th General Court gives 180 of Michael R. Knapik's votes no member code, because its roster for that court leaves him out. The sheet names him, and those votes are counted here. His party in 2013 and 2014 is not carried over from another court, so those votes enter no party rate.

The legislature's roster for the 186th General Court carries no party for anyone, and nothing is carried back from a later court, because people change parties. So no party rate above uses a vote from 2009 or 2010, and a senator who served only then shows no party. Every other rate counts those years in full.

A senator appears here only with at least 100 roll calls, and a rate is shown only where it rests on at least 60 votes. Below that a percentage is noise rather than a comparison.
