Roster notes

All rosters

Party: as elected

The party on a roster row is the party the member ran under, from the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s election results, so every row has one back to 2009. It is labelled as elected. It is not a statement of the caucus the member sat with on any given day.

Where the legislature’s own member feed lists a different party from the ballot, the roster keeps the ballot party and marks the row with an asterisk. 3 rows:

  • Thomas J. Calter, 189th General Court: feed Democrat, ballot Republican.
  • Solomon Goldstein-Rose, 190th General Court: feed Unenrolled, ballot Democrat.
  • Susannah M. Whipps, 190th General Court: feed Unenrolled, ballot Republican.

These are the changes of party the feed shows. The feed does not record every switch, so a switch it never recorded does not appear here. We have not read why any of these differ.

Leadership posts before 2017

For the 186th to 189th General Courts (2009 to 2016) the legislature’s feed names only the Speaker of the House and the President of the Senate. Floor leaders, whips and the other posts are not on file, and those cells read “not on file”. That is a gap in our sources. It does not mean the posts were empty. From the 190th the feed names 28 to 32 posts a court. Filling the earlier courts would take the Public Officers volumes and the Journals, which have not been read.

Dates served

A member who held a seat for the whole court is shown from the day the court convened (the first Wednesday of January) to the day before the next court convened. A special-election winner starts on the election date, the earliest they could have served; they were sworn in no later than their first recorded vote. A member who left mid-court ends on the date the Journal prints for the departure (the day the resignation took effect, the day of death, or the day the seat was declared vacant), shown with the reason and the Journal page. Where the Journal prints no day of leaving, the row shows the sitting at which it records the letter or the death, and says so; where it prints only a relative day (“last Saturday”), the row says the day is worked out. A member who left before the court convened is shown as having not served, with the day they left. Where no Journal prints anything, the row ends on the last vote on file, a bound set by the votes this site holds, and says it is not the date the member left. The 194th ends on its scheduled date, 5 January 2027. Where the minutes or the Journal give the real date, it is on that seat’s page.

The 13th Suffolk seat, 186th General Court

The legislature’s feed lists this seat as “Marian Walsh, MW1”. The roster row is Martin J. Walsh (MJW1). Two records agree: the Secretary of the Commonwealth’s winner for the seat, and the House sheets’ “Walsh, M.J.”, which this site’s roll call pages already tie to MJW1. Marian Walsh is the 186th Senator (code M_W0), a different person. The feed’s label is kept in the roster’s flags file. Why the feed labels the seat this way is unexplained.

188th House: three namesakes

Chris Walsh, James M. Murphy and Randy Hunt each share a surname with another member in the 188th House. The House sheets print only the surname, so the site places those 372 votes with a hand-kept table, and the roster’s count on these three rows is the count on the roll call pages. A second count, by service window, differs for the same three rows. Both counts are in the tie check kept with the roster tables. The difference is on the open list. It is not settled.

Names

Names are the legislature’s feed spellings, including nicknames the feed prints. Eight members the feed omits are named as the Secretary of the Commonwealth names them.
